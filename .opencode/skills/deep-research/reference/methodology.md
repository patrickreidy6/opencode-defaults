# Research Methodology

Use this reference when an investigation has several open questions or conflicting evidence. Adapt the approach to the question rather than executing a fixed sequence of phases.

## Frame the investigation

Identify the decision, audience, time horizon, and material constraints. State assumptions that could affect the conclusion. Organize broad questions into researchable parts; let new evidence change their priority without drifting beyond the requested scope.

Use the current date supplied by the environment for recency judgments. Choose a date range relevant to the topic; older foundational evidence may matter more than recent commentary.

## Retrieve useful evidence

- Prefer original research, official records, datasets, and documentation for claims they directly establish. Use secondary sources for context, interpretation, and leads to primary material.
- Use available search and retrieval tools. Context7 is appropriate for current library and API documentation. Follow each tool's actual schema.
- Parallelize independent queries when useful; follow-up searches should respond to evidence already found. Agent delegation is optional and follows the user's request and applicable project instructions.
- Inspect underlying sources before relying on them. Search snippets and summaries are leads, not substitutes for the evidence.
- Expand retrieval where missing evidence could change the answer. A quota of sources, search angles, or source types does not determine adequacy.

## Keep claims traceable

Track enough provenance to recover the evidence:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Claim or question | What this source supports or challenges |
| URL, title, author or organization | Identifies the original source |
| Publication/update date and access date | Establishes temporal relevance |
| Passage, data, or location | Allows the claim to be checked in context |
| Limitations or conflicts | Records what the evidence cannot establish |

Use working notes for a short investigation. Persist an evidence record alongside a saved report when the work needs to survive compaction or handoff; see [continuation](continuation.md).

## Evaluate and synthesize

Assess directness, methodology, independence, recency, and incentives. Several articles repeating one announcement are one evidence chain, not independent corroboration. Numerical credibility scores are optional heuristics, not calibrated probabilities.

Corroborate disputed or consequential claims where possible. A single authoritative source may establish a narrow fact; broader causal or comparative claims often need more evidence. Describe conflicts in terms of differences in dates, definitions, populations, methods, or incentives rather than forcing agreement.

Connect findings to the user's decision. Distinguish source-supported facts from your analysis and identify what evidence would change the recommendation.

## Finish

Continue targeted retrieval if an unresolved issue could change the conclusion. Deliver once the scoped questions are addressed, supporting evidence is traceable, and remaining limits are explicit. If access or time prevents adequate coverage, identify the specific gap and its effect on the answer instead of presenting partial work as complete.
